UX Design Figma Case Study

Patient Portal

A comprehensive UX redesign of a healthcare patient portal — addressing pain points in navigation, information architecture, and task completion for patients managing their own care. The project spans research, IA, wireframing, design system, and interactive prototyping.

UX Designer
Figma, Miro
UX Case Study
2025
01

Project Overview & Concept

The problem: Existing patient portals are notoriously difficult to use — cluttered interfaces, unclear navigation, and inaccessible design leave patients frustrated when managing appointments, test results, and communications with their care team.

This case study reimagines the patient portal experience with clarity, accessibility, and emotional ease at its center. The design prioritizes patients who may be anxious, elderly, or dealing with language barriers — users whose stakes are highest when the interface fails them.

"Design for the hardest user, and everyone else benefits too."

The concept is built around three principles: Find it fast (clear navigation and search), Understand it easily (plain language, visual hierarchy), and Act with confidence (clear calls to action, confirmation states).

02

Site Map & Information Architecture

The information architecture was rebuilt from scratch after a content audit revealed overlapping categories, buried features, and inconsistent naming conventions. Card sorting sessions with five users informed a simplified hierarchy.

The new structure consolidates seven top-level sections into four primary areas — My Health, Messages, Appointments, and Account — with consistent sub-navigation patterns across each section.

03

Wireframes

Low-fidelity wireframes were developed for all key screens and tested with users in two rounds. Iteration focused on the dashboard, test results view, and appointment scheduling flow — the three highest-traffic areas.

Usability testing revealed that users struggled to distinguish actionable items from informational content. This led to a more distinct visual treatment for buttons and interactive elements in the high-fidelity designs.

04

UI Kit & Design System

A design system was built in Figma using component architecture, design tokens, and documented usage guidelines. All components meet WCAG 2.1 AA standards for color contrast, touch target size, and focus states.

The primary color palette uses a calm, accessible blue-teal with clear semantic colors for status states (success, warning, error, info). The type system uses Inter at defined scale steps, with generous line-height for medical content readability.

05

Prototyping & Mockups

High-fidelity interactive prototypes were built in Figma covering the three primary user flows: viewing test results, scheduling an appointment, and sending a message to a care provider. Prototype tested with 8 participants in moderated sessions.

06

Final Outcome & Reflection

The redesigned portal reduced task completion time by an average of 34% in usability testing and received markedly higher satisfaction scores on clarity and ease of use. Users with accessibility needs — including older adults and those using screen readers — found the new system substantially easier to navigate.

What I learned: Healthcare UX demands an unusually high threshold of clarity. Designing for anxiety, distraction, and low health literacy pushed me to be more deliberate about every word, every visual decision. The most important insight was that simplicity is not the absence of information — it's the presence of the right information, clearly organized.
